Project 01 / Embedded Hardware + Firmware

White Lightning.

A custom vehicle controller from circuit design to physical testing.

ESP32C++KiCadArduinoMPU6050

Montana State University · 2025

The Challenge

White Lightning was a three-person engineering project to develop a compact, remotely operated racing vehicle. The team iterated through multiple mechanical prototypes. I owned the circuit design and wrote the controller firmware, using AI to help interpret documentation and ESP32 integration practices.

My Contributions

01 / ElectronicsDesigned the custom KiCad schematic and PCB layout, including development-board and IMU module interfaces, drivetrain and servo connectors, FPV driver interface, and mounting geometry.
02 / FirmwareImplemented Bluetooth gamepad input, output mapping for the drivetrain and steering assembly, a dynamic calibration system, and a customizable IMU-based traction control system.
03 / ValidationIntegrated hardware and software with the chassis and performed extensive physical testing of the controller functionality and driver-assist features.

System Integration

The controller uses an ESP32 development board, an MPU6050 motion-sensing module, and dedicated connectors for battery/ESC power, steering servo, and ESC commands. The board integrates existing modules; it is not a custom microcontroller design.

Control Software

The Arduino/C++ controller handles gamepad connectivity and drivetrain output. IMU data is calibrated and used to modulate control commands under detected dynamic conditions. The resulting controller was tested extensively on test bench and later implemented into the chassis for further validation.

Prototyping and Reflection

Vehicle testing informed revised wheel geometry, tire compound, and chassis rigidity. The project reinforced the importance of integration boundaries: mechanical design, electronics, and control software each had to work together. It also highlighted future opportunities for dedicated failsafe logic, deterministic loop scheduling, and more rigorous performance measurement.

Three-person team: Camden Roberts-Hurd (electronic design and firmware development), Christopher Jones and Samuel Smith (mechanical chassis and drivetrain development).
